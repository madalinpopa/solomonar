## A rider of storms

In Romanian folklore, the **solomonar** is a learned figure who rides a dragon and controls the weather. Accounts vary: the mount may be called a *zmeu* or a *balaur*, and the stories include a school, a book of knowledge, and reins used to summon and guide the dragon.

These are elements of a living folklore tradition, not a single fixed story. See [the Solomonari overview and its historical sources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solomonari).

## From folklore to a framework

The project takes that imagery as a metaphor for deliberate work with AI coding agents:

- **The book becomes specifications:** a shared account of the agreed behavior.
- **The spells become skills:** focused procedures for discovery, specification, and task preparation.
- **The dragons become agents:** implementation and independent review with distinct responsibilities.
- **The reins become orchestration:** coordination, decisions, and a clear path through the checks.

This mapping belongs to Solomonar the project. It is a design interpretation, rather than a claim about the historical meaning of every folkloric detail.

## Cusătura: the stitch

The project's logo is an S-shaped balaur made from cross-stitches. Its palette pairs red thread with ink and warm cloth, connecting Romanian embroidery to the precision of a pixel grid.

Traditional Romanian and Moldovan blouse embroidery combines horizontal, vertical, and diagonal stitches, with geometric and organic ornamentation that varies by region and maker. [UNESCO's description of the blouse with altiță](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/the-art-of-the-traditional-blouse-with-embroidery-on-the-shoulder-alti-an-element-of-cultural-identity-in-romania-and-the-republic-of-moldova-01861) provides the cultural context.

The website borrows the visual language of the existing logo: cross-stitches, repeated geometric borders, red thread, and cloth. Its decorative patterns are contemporary artwork inspired by that language. They do not reproduce a named regional pattern or claim a universal meaning for a motif.

## Two themes, one thread

The dark theme uses charcoal cloth, ivory ink, and coral thread. The light theme uses warm linen, dark ink, and deep red thread. The dragon is the same source mark in both.

The illustrated story moves from the scholar and book to spells, the dragon ride, and a shared direction. It can be paused or explored one chapter at a time, and respects reduced-motion preferences. The visual story should help you find the documentation, never stand between you and it.
